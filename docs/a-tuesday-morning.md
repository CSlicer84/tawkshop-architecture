# A Tuesday morning

*What the loop actually does, on one account. The company is invented. The mechanics are not.*

---

The README argues that context quality is the product. That can be read, agreed with, and still leave you without a picture of what happens when you open the thing. This is that picture, kept short.

The account is Northgate Financial. Partner-led, eleven months old, a champion who moved internally, a close date that has moved twice.

## Before the meeting

I ask for a brief. What comes back is not the CRM record reformatted, because that record is forty fields and most were last touched by me in a hurry.

Three parts of it do not exist in a CRM.

**What is thin.** The economic buyer is asserted but never evidenced. A name appeared in a transcript in March, nobody confirmed the budget sits with them, nothing since has tested it.

**What has gone stale.** The close date was set on a procurement timeline given by someone who left in August. The date is not wrong. It has stopped having a reason behind it, and it is shown differently from a fact confirmed last week.

**What is contested.** Two sources disagree about whether the incumbent is still in the deal. The system does not resolve that for me. It shows both, with their evidence and their dates, and marks it unresolved. Which tells me what to ask.

## After the meeting

The transcript is stored as an artefact and gets an identifier, because everything downstream has to cite it.

The analysis layer reads it and returns a proposal. Not a write. It is validated against a published contract of writable paths and permitted vocabulary, then rendered for me: a new stakeholder, a close date moving, the departed champion retired, the incumbent question resolved.

Each change carries its evidence. Mostly a quotation long enough that I can check the reading without opening the source. Where the model concluded something nobody said, it is declared an inference and recorded as one rather than dressed up as a quote.

## The review, which is the part that matters

Three moves, and the middle one is the common one.

I approve most as proposed. The close date I amend, because the model read March from the procurement step and missed that their change freeze runs through March too. My correction is re-validated against the same contract the model's proposal had to satisfy.

One I discard, and the discard is recorded rather than silently dropped, because a model that keeps proposing the same discarded thing is telling me something.

Then a deterministic engine applies the result. No model at that step, which is what makes it reproducible.

## Afterwards

The close date now names me as its author, because I corrected it. The ones I approved as proposed name the model as proposer and me as authoriser. That distinction is the difference between a record that can tell you how a value came to be and one that can only tell you what it currently says.

The old date is superseded rather than overwritten, so the original and its expired reasoning are both still there.

Next Tuesday the brief is built from this. The incumbent question is closed. The economic buyer is still thin, because nothing on this call tested it, and the system will say so again.
